# Usage tips shown at startup and via Help > Usage Tips (#211).
# Each tip: a title line, then its body (simple HTML allowed). Tips are separated by a line of three dashes.
# Lines starting with # are comments. Keep each tip short; update these with each release.
---
Create profiles straight from your identity provider
Open <i>File &rarr; Manage Profiles...</i> and choose <b>Import from IdP...</b>. Log in once and every AWS role you're granted is listed; tick the ones you want and they become profiles. No account numbers or role names to look up.
---
Pin the profiles you use most
Right-click a row in the status table and choose <b>Pin Profile</b>, or drag a row to the top. Pinned profiles stay at the top of the table whatever the filter, and appear in the tray menu.
---
Work from the tray
With the window closed, the tray icon's menu has each pinned profile with its status, plus <b>Request Credentials</b>, <b>Open Console</b> and <b>Refresh Expiring/Expired Profiles</b>. Results arrive as notifications.
---
Right-click for quick actions
Right-click any row in the status table to request or show credentials, open the AWS Console, <b>check identity</b> (ask AWS which role the credentials really are), copy the account number, pin the profile, or delete it.
---
Paste credentials straight into a terminal
<b>Show Credentials</b> has a <i>Format</i> menu for bash/zsh, PowerShell, Command Prompt and plain <code>.env</code> lines. <b>Copy to Clipboard</b> copies just the commands, ready to paste.
---
Refresh several profiles at once
<i>Actions &rarr; Refresh Expiring/Expired Profiles...</i> renews everything that has run out or is about to. To have pinned profiles renewed for you, turn on <b>auto-renew</b> in <i>File &rarr; Configuration</i>.
---
Find a profile quickly
Type in the <b>Filter</b> box above the status table to narrow it by profile or GUI name, and use the status checkboxes to show only valid, expired or unknown profiles. Click a column header to sort; right-click one to show the <b>GUI Name</b> column.
---
Move to a new machine, or share a setup
<i>File &rarr; Export Profiles...</i> saves your profiles and settings to a file; <i>Import Profiles...</i> loads it elsewhere. The file never contains credentials or passwords.
---
Use the AWS CLI right away
After you request credentials, the profile's region is added to <code>~/.aws/config</code> if it has none, so <code>aws --profile &lt;name&gt; ...</code> just works. You can turn this off in <i>File &rarr; Configuration</i>.
---
Keyboard shortcuts
<b>Ctrl+P</b> opens Manage Profiles, <b>Ctrl+,</b> opens Configuration, and <b>Ctrl+Q</b> exits. Menus and buttons also have underlined Alt-key mnemonics.
---
Something not working?
<i>Help &rarr; View Logs...</i> shows what the app has been doing, including a startup log if it ever fails to open. <b>Open Log Folder</b> takes you to the files to share when asking for help.
---
Read the status colors
In the status table, the Status column is <b>green</b> while credentials are valid, <b>amber</b> when they're about to expire (5 minutes before, unless you change it in <i>File &rarr; Configuration</i>), <b>red</b> once expired, and grey when the app doesn't know.
---
Get a heads-up before credentials expire
Turn on <i>Show tray notification before credentials expire</i> in <i>File &rarr; Configuration</i> and you'll get a tray notification shortly before a profile runs out, even with the window closed. Choose how early with <i>Warn when credentials expire within</i>; a longer warning helps before a long meeting.
---
Refresh just the profiles you pick
Select several rows in the status table with <b>Ctrl</b>-click or <b>Shift</b>-click, then choose <i>Actions &rarr; Refresh Selected Profiles...</i>.
---
One login, many profiles
When refreshing several profiles at once, the app logs in once per identity provider and reuses that login for every profile that shares it, so you aren't asked to sign in over and over.
---
Changed your mind? Cancel
While a login is running, the <b>Request Credentials</b> button becomes <b>Cancel</b>, and a batch refresh shows <b>Cancel Batch Refresh</b>. The browser is closed for you.
---
Watch the login happen
Tick <b>Show browser</b> before requesting credentials to see the login window, for example to complete an MFA prompt or see where a login is getting stuck. Afterwards the browser stays signed in to the console, on the profile's <i>Open Console to</i> page. Batch refreshes always run in the background.
---
Skip typing your password
In <i>File &rarr; Configuration &rarr; Password Storage</i>, <b>Store and use Okta password</b> keeps your password encrypted on this computer for as long as you choose. <b>Forget Password</b> clears it at any time.
---
Sign in with Okta FastPass
If your organization uses Okta Verify, turn on <b>Use Okta FastPass</b> in <i>File &rarr; Configuration</i> to sign in with your device instead of a password.
---
Choose how long credentials last
Set the session length in <i>File &rarr; Configuration &rarr; Session</i>. AWS won't issue credentials for longer than the role's maximum session duration in IAM, so ask your AWS admin if you need more.
---
Light or dark
Pick a theme in <i>File &rarr; Configuration &rarr; Appearance</i>. There are several light and dark themes, and the change applies immediately.
---
Use the browser you prefer
Logins can run in Chrome, Firefox or Edge. Choose one in <i>File &rarr; Configuration &rarr; Browser</i>.
---
Keep it running in the background
Closing the window doesn't quit the app; it keeps watching your credentials from the system tray. Turn on <i>Start minimized to tray</i> to skip the window at launch, and use <i>File &rarr; Exit</i> or the tray menu to quit.
---
Several AWS consoles side by side
<b>Open Console</b> opens each profile in its own browser window with its own cookies, so you can be signed in to different accounts at the same time. Give a profile a <i>Region</i> and choose a service under <i>Open Console to</i> in <i>Manage Profiles &rarr; Edit</i>, and it opens right where you work.
---
Hand credentials to a script safely
Put a <code>public_key.pem</code> in your <code>~/.aws</code> folder and the <b>Encrypted</b> button gives you the current credentials as an encrypted string that only the matching private key can read.
---
Prefer editing files?
Profiles live in <code>~/.aws/samlsts</code>, a plain INI file. You can edit it by hand; the app picks up changes on its next status refresh (every 30 seconds) or when you click <b>Refresh Status</b>.
---
Tidy up removed profiles
Profiles you delete from <code>samlsts</code> drop out of the status table after a short grace period. To remove them right away, use <b>Force Refresh</b> in <i>File &rarr; Configuration &rarr; Data</i>.
---
Try again with one click
When a login times out or hits a temporary problem, the error message offers <b>Retry</b>, so you don't have to start over.
---
See what the login page showed
If a login step fails, the app saves a screenshot of the page to <code>~/.aws/login-failure-screenshots</code>. It's handy for seeing an unexpected prompt or for sharing when asking for help.
---
Stay up to date
The app checks for new versions in the background and lets you know when one is available. <i>Help &rarr; What's New</i> shows every release, and <i>Help &rarr; Release Notes</i> shows what changed in yours.
